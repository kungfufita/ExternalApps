# Opportunity Research — Candidate Businesses

Desk research scoring candidate avenues of business against the platform's
[opportunity scoring model](../docs/06-opportunity-scoring.md). Scores are
1–5 per factor (higher always better; "less is better" factors are inverted),
weighted to a total out of 100.

> **Status: desk-research estimates (August 2026).** These are hypotheses to
> be tested, not verified facts — stage 1 (Validate) of any chartered venture
> exists to confirm or kill them. Re-score quarterly. The interactive scoreboard
> with adjustable weights lives in the [platform console](../console/index.html).

## Candidate notes

### 1 · Digital products & templates — 85
Spreadsheets, Notion systems, printables, design assets, niche toolkits sold on
marketplaces or a own storefront. **Why it scores:** near-zero marginal cost
(margins effectively 90%+), fully automatable delivery, starts for almost
nothing, and a first sale can happen in weeks. **Weaknesses:** every sale is
one-off (R2) and marketplaces are crowded (B2) — winning requires a sharply
specific niche and consistent output. **Risk to validate:** discoverability;
one product proves nothing, a catalog with a channel does.

### 2 · Productized AI service — 80
A fixed-scope, fixed-price service (report generation, data cleanup, content
operations, bookkeeping-lite for a specific niche) with an AI-first pipeline and
human review — rung 3 of the automation ladder by design. **Why it scores:**
high automation ceiling, subscription pricing, fast to first customer, and
demand is broad and current. **Weaknesses:** quality control is the whole game;
scores assume a niche where errors are cheap to catch. **Risk to validate:**
willingness to pay for output without the reassurance of a human-hours invoice.

### 3 · Niche micro-SaaS — 77
A small software tool solving one painful workflow for one specific industry.
**Why it scores:** best-in-class margins and recurring revenue; once stable,
runs at rung 4 with support as the main human load. **Weaknesses:** the least
simple option here (S2) — building, hosting, support, and churn are all real —
and revenue arrives slowly (V2). **Risk to validate:** the niche's pain must be
sharp enough to pay monthly for; validate with a concierge/manual version first.

### 4 · Online course / cohort — 73
Teach a monetizable skill you can credibly claim. **Why it scores:** 90%+
margins, trivial capital, and delivery automates well (evergreen format).
**Weaknesses:** one-off purchases unless converted to a membership; crowded.
**Situational fit:** strong only where the platform has a genuine, demonstrable
expertise — otherwise it's a content-marketing grind.

### 5 · Self-storage — 70
Unmanned facility with smart locks, cameras, and automated billing. **Why it
scores:** the most automatable physical business there is, sticky recurring
revenue, durable demand. **Weaknesses:** heavy capital (C1) and slow to first
dollar (V1) — land, build or convert, permit. **Situational fit:** becomes the
top pick if capital is available and patient; a REIT-adjacent hold, not a hustle.

### 6 · Newsletter / content site — 68
Niche audience monetized by sponsors, affiliates, and eventually products.
**Why it scores:** simple, nearly free to start, compounding asset.
**Weaknesses:** slow (V2), only partially automatable (voice is the product),
and monetization per reader is modest. **Best use on this platform:** as the
audience engine that de-risks candidates 1, 2, and 4 rather than as a
standalone venture.

### 7 · Automated car wash — 66
Self-serve or tunnel wash with app payment and membership plans. High automation
(A5) and real margins once running, but capital-heavy (C1) with siting and
permitting complexity. Membership models have made revenue stickier. Situational:
needs capital and a good location to exist at all.

### 8 · Print-on-demand storefront — 65
Design-led merch with third-party fulfillment. Fully automated fulfillment and
near-zero capital, but thin margins after fulfillment fees (P2), no recurrence,
and brutal competition (B1). Viable as a low-effort side avenue attached to an
audience (see #6), weak as a standalone bet.

### 9 · Smart vending routes — 65
Card-payment machines with telemetry for restock routing. Semi-passive, simple,
proven. Middling economics per machine mean it scales by fleet, and each machine
is capital plus placement negotiation. Honest, unglamorous, situational.

### 10 · Remote-managed cleaning company — 63
Booking software + vetted subcontractors + review loop; the operator manages the
system, not the mop. Fast to revenue and naturally recurring, but automation
tops out low (A2 — quality problems are human problems) and margins compress
with subcontractor pay. Workforce-heavy by design — the inverse of the
platform's automation bias, which the score reflects.

### 11 · Laundromat — 62
Classic semi-absentee cash flow with card systems and remote monitoring. Durable
demand and recurring use, but expensive to enter (C1), slow to start, and
equipment maintenance is the hidden workload. Situational: an acquisition of an
existing underpriced store scores better than a greenfield build.

### 12 · Short-term rental management — 58
Managing other owners' properties for a fee (no property capital needed at the
management-company angle). Recurring and in demand, but operationally complex
(S2): guest issues, turnovers, and regulatory churn in many cities. Automation
helps messaging and pricing, not toilets.

### 13 · E-commerce private label — 51
Sourcing a product, branding it, selling on marketplaces. Inventory risk,
capital tied up in stock, fierce competition (B1), and platform-dependency.
Passes only with a genuinely differentiated product — re-score if one appears.

## What the ranking says

The top of the board is dominated by **digital, high-margin, automation-friendly**
businesses — consistent with the scoring model's weights, and a fair reflection
of the platform's bias. Two portfolio observations:

1. **A sensible opening portfolio is #1 + #2 + #6:** digital products for fast
   cash flow, a productized AI service for recurring revenue, and a newsletter
   as the shared audience engine feeding both. All three start for under ~$1k
   combined and share one content/automation toolchain.
2. **The capital-heavy physical plays (#5, #7, #11) are a different game** —
   they reward patient capital, not iteration speed. Park them until a digital
   venture is generating the capital, then re-score.
