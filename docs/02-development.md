# Pillar 2 — Development

How a venture moves from idea to operating business, and how it keeps improving.

## Stages

Every venture is always in exactly one stage, recorded in its `roadmap.md`.

| Stage | Goal | Exit criterion |
|---|---|---|
| **0 — Idea** | State the opportunity in one page | An owner commits to it |
| **1 — Validate** | Prove someone will pay / use it | First real customer or committed user |
| **2 — Build** | Deliver the smallest sellable version | Revenue or usage is repeatable, not one-off |
| **3 — Operate** | Run it reliably and profitably | Unit economics positive; playbook written |
| **4 — Scale or Sustain** | Grow it, or hold a healthy steady state | Reviewed quarterly |

Rules of thumb:

- Don't build in stage 1 more than you need to validate. Sell it before you perfect it.
- A venture can be sent **back** a stage when reality disagrees with the plan; that's
  the system working, not failing.
- Stage changes are logged with a date and one sentence of justification.

## Development practices

- **One roadmap per venture** (`roadmap.md`): now / next / later. Short. Honest.
- **Small increments** — ship the smallest change that teaches you something or earns
  something. This applies to products, services, and internal process alike.
- **Write down what you learn** — a validated assumption, a dead end, a supplier who
  came through. The platform's compounding advantage is retained learning across
  ventures.
- **Technical work follows the platform's engineering defaults** — version control for
  anything built, review before it reaches customers, and automation of anything done
  more than twice (see Pillar 5).

## Market development

Product development and market development advance together:

- Every stage has a customer-facing question attached (Will they take a call? Will they
  pay? Will they come back? Will they refer?).
- Channels are developed deliberately: pick one channel per stage, make it work or
  rule it out, then add the next.
