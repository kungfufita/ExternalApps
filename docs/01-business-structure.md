# Pillar 1 — Business Structure

How ventures are set up, owned, and governed, and how they relate to the platform.

## Platform model

The platform acts as the umbrella: shared identity, shared framework, shared tooling.
Each venture is a distinct avenue of business under that umbrella. Three structural
patterns are supported:

1. **Internal line of business** — the venture is an activity of the parent entity.
   Simplest to start; suits early-stage or low-risk avenues.
2. **Separate entity** — the venture is its own legal entity (company/LLC or local
   equivalent), owned wholly or partly by the parent. Suits ventures with real
   liability exposure, outside investors, or partners.
3. **Joint venture / partnership** — shared ownership with an external party,
   governed by a written agreement. Suits ventures built on someone else's channel,
   asset, or expertise.

A venture starts as pattern 1 by default and graduates to 2 or 3 when a documented
trigger is hit (liability, investment, partner equity, regulatory need).

> **Note:** entity choice, tax, and liability questions need professional legal and
> accounting advice for the relevant jurisdiction. This document organizes the
> decision; it doesn't replace that advice.

## Roles every venture must fill

- **Owner** — accountable for the venture end to end. Exactly one person.
- **Operator** — runs day-to-day. May be the owner early on.
- **Platform sponsor** — the platform-side contact who unblocks shared resources.

One person may hold multiple roles in a small venture, but every role must have a
named holder in the venture's `operations.md`.

## Decision-making

- **Venture-local decisions** (pricing, hiring within budget, tooling within the
  approved list) are made by the venture owner.
- **Platform decisions** (new entity formation, cross-venture resource shifts,
  brand use, anything binding the parent) are made at the platform level.
- Decisions worth remembering are recorded briefly in the venture's `operations.md`
  under "Decision log" — one line each: date, decision, why.

## Inter-venture rules

- Ventures may trade with each other; do it at arm's length and write it down.
- Shared costs (tools, people split across ventures) are allocated explicitly so
  each venture's true economics stay visible.
- A venture's failure must not be able to take another venture down — no informal
  cross-guarantees, shared bank accounts, or commingled obligations.
